Hair Color Changer - AI Try On

Privacy Policy

Effective 2026-11-07

In short: no account, no ads, no analytics, no tracking. A photo leaves your phone only when you allow AI processing and tap Try this look; it goes to Replicate and Google to make your look, and we do not keep it.

Who we are

Hair Color Changer - AI Try On (“the app”) is made by Swapnil Jain (“we”, “us”). Questions about privacy: business@firstimpresslabs.com.

What stays on your phone

These are stored on your device and are not sent to us:

  • Your onboarding answers (which styles to show, where to open) and your settings
  • Whether you allowed photos to be sent for AI processing
  • How many looks you have made (kept in your phone's keychain)
  • Your purchase status

Before anything is sent, the app checks on your phone, with Apple's on-device Vision framework, that the photo shows one face; no face data is stored or sent. The app does not keep your photos or your looks: they stay in memory until you save them to Photos, share them, change the photo or close the app.

What the app asks to use

Camera. The app uses the camera to take a selfie to try looks on, when you choose to. A photo is sent for AI processing only as described below.

Photos. You choose a photo with the system photo picker, which gives the app only that photo. When you tap Save, the app asks for permission to add the look to your library. When you pick a photo, Apple's photo picker gives the app only the photos you choose. The app cannot see the rest of your library. To save an image, the app asks only for permission to add photos to your library, which does not let it see your photos.

What we do not collect

Apart from what the AI feature sends, as described below, we do not collect your name, email address, phone number, contacts, payment card details, browsing or search history, usage analytics or crash data. We do not ask for or use the advertising identifier.

Photos you send for AI processing

Only after you allow it in the app, and only when you tap Try this look, the app sends the photo you chose (made smaller first, about 1,000 pixels on its longest side, JPEG, with its photo metadata removed) and the shade and cut you picked to our server, hosted on Cloudflare, which passes it to Replicate and Google to produce the result.

  • Replicate: receives the request and runs it on Google's image model. (privacy policy)
  • Google: runs the AI model (Gemini image, "Nano Banana") that redraws your hair in the shade or cut you chose. (privacy policy)

The new look is downloaded to your phone from the address the AI service returns.

Our server passes the request to Replicate and the result back to the app. We do not keep a copy of your photos or your looks. Like any web host, Cloudflare sees the IP address of each request.

Replicate deletes the inputs and outputs of predictions made through its API about an hour after they finish. Google's terms for its model allow it to keep inputs for up to 55 days, for abuse monitoring.

Who processes it

  • Cloudflare hosts the server that passes your photo to the AI provider, as our processor.
  • Replicate processes the photos you send, as described above.
  • Google processes the photos you send, as described above.
  • Apple handles all payments. We never see your card details.

Apple handles its services under its own privacy policy: apple.com/legal/privacy.

What we do not do

  • We do not sell or share your data, and we do not use it for advertising.
  • We do not track you across other companies' apps or websites.
  • The app contains no ads and no analytics or crash-reporting SDKs.

Your choices

  • Allow or remove access to the camera and your photos in the Settings app at any time.

Everything the app keeps is deleted when you delete the app, except the looks counter in the keychain, which iOS may keep until you erase the phone. It holds only dates and counts.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from them.

Changes

If this policy changes, we will update this page and its effective date. A change that collects more data will also be described in the app's release notes.
